No. 01

Artificial Creativity: Standing On The Shoulders Of Algorithms

Dr. Ciarán Trolan

Consultant Geriatrician

Poem diptych

Both poems are revisions. Reinterpretations of works of others, so really artificial creativity. One in English, one as Gaeilge; an inflexion of way of saying, ways of being and the strength in having skills and knowledge that straddle the new and old.

Fear Lasta lampAI

1. The first, “Fear Lasta lampAI” is a revision of Máirtín Ó Direáin’s poem. MÓD was from Inis Mór, a career civil servant, “Stoite” or being uprooted from Gaelic bucolic life and transplantation in the urban modernity was the theme of his poetry; a form of Gaelic revivalism that ran headlong into the apocalyptic sense of civilisation being WW1 and WW2.

Artificial Intelligence is considered by many to be apocalyptic for societies as they are currently structured. Clinicians and healthcare will need to innovate.

AI I AM: Bani Adam for the New Age

2. This second in the diptych is “AI I AM: Bani Adam for the New Age”. It is a revision of 13th century Persian poet Saadi Shirazi’s epic Gulistan/The Rose Garden. He was considered to have a humanistic, inclusive worldview and focussed on empathy. Also a civil servant.

It is a 10/10/10/10/11/10 rhyming couplet metre, common in Farsi language poetry of the time. The poem still appears on the 100,000-rial note in modern day Iran. New Dervishes, new Plutocrats; same old story.

Leib and Körper both translate to “body” in English, but represent a crucial German philosophical distinction: Leib is the lived, subjective, and experienced body (“the body I am”), while Körper refers to the physical, objective, and anatomical body (“the body I have”). Leib implies personhood and vitality, often used in phenomenology, whereas Körper refers to a scientific object.

Clinicians of the future will need to keep the light shining on Leib even if AI may treat us as Körper.
